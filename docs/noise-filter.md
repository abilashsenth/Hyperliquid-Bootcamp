# Noise filter

The rule used for every candidate: keep it if it explains a mechanism or a skill and the author gains nothing from your trading; drop it if the first thing it wants is your sign-up, your subscription, or your belief in a price. Below is what that rule removed on 3 November 2026, by pattern, with the specific example that was found.

| Pattern | What was found and dropped | Tell |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Referral-first guides | hyperliquidguide.com: a thorough 2026 beginner guide that states it earns from its referral code and from affiliate links on wallets, tax software and TradingView. Coin Bureau's guide: a media brand funded by exchange referral deals. | The first call to action is a code or a sign-up link. The content may be fine; the incentive is to get you trading, not trading well. |
| Unvetted YouTube tutorials | Five "Hyperliquid for beginners" videos (channels: Crypto4light Trading, The Blockchain Today, Lukas Huettis, Greeny, University of Crypto). Descriptions could not be read by the checker, and this category is dominated by referral codes. | Read the description before the video. A referral code, a "join my group" link, or a thumbnail with a dollar figure means skip. |
| Marketing dressed as explainers | Nansen's "What is HIP-3?" post: accurate in outline, but it pitches Nansen's product throughout and frames everything as HYPE demand. The HIP-3 docs page says the same with no pitch. | The explainer ends with the vendor's dashboard as the answer. |

## The general categories to avoid, whatever the source

- Paid signal groups, "VIP" Telegram or Discord channels, and anyone who sells entries.
- "Turned $100 into $10k" videos, PnL screenshots without the losing trades, and leverage flexes.
- Copy-trading vaults promoted by the vault leader, and "whale alert" accounts that exist to move thin coins.
- Points-farming and airdrop threads. They optimise volume, which is the opposite of what a learner needs.
- Price-target accounts with no invalidation level. A call without a level that proves it wrong is content, not analysis.
- AI-generated "reports" and summary sites. Read the primary source they summarise.

## A 60-second vetting routine

1. Who pays the author? If it is you (subscription) or the exchange (referral), weigh it accordingly.
2. Does it cite the docs, the chain, or a dataset? If it cites nothing, it is opinion.
3. Is there a number you can check in two minutes? Check it against the docs or the explorer.
4. Does the piece name what would make it wrong? If not, it is marketing.
5. Is it older than the last protocol change it touches? Hyperliquid changes monthly; the docs are the only always-current source.
